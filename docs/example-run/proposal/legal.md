Objective:
Prepare Legal's clause-review input to the AI-readiness RFP response and SOW for a regional hospital network, flagging contractual terms that create unpriced obligations before the engagement lead finalizes and signs. This is preparatory analysis only — not legal advice, and nothing here approves the document for signature.

Body:

1. **Scope/Statement of Work — "readiness assessment, not a build" language (SOW §1, per Pre-Sales draft).** Obligation: limits deliverables to assessment and roadmap. Concern: if the RFP's evaluation criteria or the hospital's own use-case language implies measured clinical or financial outcomes, there's a gap between what's promised and what's scored against. Redline: add explicit disclaimer that no outcome, accuracy, or ROI figure is warranted by this phase, and that any clinical-safety claims require separate validation.

2. **Data access and PHI handling (Data Room / Access clause, likely SOW Appendix on data access).** Obligation: the network will grant access to clinical, EHR, and claims data for the assessment. Concern: this is regulated health data under HIPAA. No Business Associate Agreement (BAA) is referenced in the teammate draft. Without a BAA, any access to PHI — even for read-only assessment — creates exposure for both parties. **This item is escalated to counsel; I do not opine on HIPAA compliance or enforceability.** Redline direction: SOW must reference and incorporate a signed BAA as a condition precedent to any data access, not a parallel or "as-needed" document.

3. **IP and work-product ownership (typical SOW IP clause).** Obligation: standard consulting IP clauses often assign "all deliverables and materials created" to the client, sometimes sweeping in pre-existing frameworks, readiness-assessment templates, or clause libraries the firm brings to every engagement. Cost if unaddressed: loss of reusable IP across future clients, or a dispute over what the firm can reuse next engagement. Redline: carve out pre-existing IP and background tools by name in an exhibit; assign only client-specific deliverables (the roadmap document, findings specific to this network).

4. **Liability cap and indemnification (MSA, not visible in this draft — flag for review when MSA is attached).** Standard commercial engagements cap liability at fees paid; readiness engagements touching PHI sometimes carry uncapped indemnities for data breach or unauthorized disclosure. Cost if uncapped: exposure not bounded by the engagement value — for a fee of, say, $150,000 [ASSUMPTION: illustrative engagement size, not sourced from the RFP], an uncapped breach indemnity has no ceiling. Redline: cap liability at a multiple of fees (standard is 1–2x), with a separate, narrower carve-out for confidentiality/PHI breach if the client insists — that carve-out itself should still be capped, and its cap is a negotiation point, not something Legal sets alone.

5. **Acceptance criteria for the roadmap deliverable.** If the SOW says the roadmap is "accepted" upon delivery vs. upon client sign-off against undefined criteria, subjective acceptance language lets the client withhold payment indefinitely. Redline: define acceptance as delivery of the agreed roadmap format within 5 business days review window, deemed accepted if no written objection.

6. **Termination rights.** Watch for one-way termination-for-convenience favoring only the hospital network with no notice-period or wind-down payment for work completed. Redline: mutual termination-for-convenience with payment for work performed to date.

Risks:

- **Worth negotiating:** Uncapped or unclear liability/indemnity terms; IP sweep-in of pre-existing tooling; subjective acceptance criteria.
- **Acceptable, noted:** One-sided but standard confidentiality survival clauses (typical 3–5 year term); standard governing-law clause assuming it names the hospital's home jurisdiction (jurisdiction not yet confirmed in task — flagged, not resolved, here).
